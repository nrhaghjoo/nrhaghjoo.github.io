About
Bridging AI
and Biology

I am a Postdoctoral Researcher at the Texas Biomedical Research Institute in the Host-Pathogen Interaction department, working in Dr. Diako Ebrahimi's Lab. My research sits at the intersection of artificial intelligence, statistics, and mathematics — applied to fundamental biological questions in HIV and cancer.

With a Ph.D. in Bioinformatics and Systems Biology from the University of Tehran, and nearly a decade of combined academic and industry experience, I develop computational tools and analytical pipelines that extract biological insight from complex multi-omics and sequencing data. I am particularly interested in mutational signatures, protein evolution, and machine learning-based biomarker discovery.

Current Research

Host-Pathogen Interactions

2024 –
present

APOBEC3 Protein Sector Analysis

Texas Biomedical Research Institute · Ebrahimi Lab

Applying statistical coupling analysis (SCA) and co-evolutionary methods to identify functional sectors within the APOBEC3 family of proteins — enzymes central to both antiviral restriction and cancer mutagenesis. This work aims to decode how co-evolving residue networks encode distinct biological functions including HIV restriction and tumor mutational burden.

  • Trinucleotide-context mutation analysis across viral sequence alignments
  • Deep learning for antimicrobial peptide identification from gut microbiome data
  • Integration of evolutionary conservation and co-variation signals
