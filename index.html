Current Research

Two diseases.
One question: why here?

At Dr. Diako Ebrahimi's Lab at the Texas Biomedical Research Institute, I study the APOBEC3 family of proteins — enzymes that sit at the crossroads of antiviral defence and cancer mutagenesis. Understanding them means understanding both HIV restriction and how tumours accumulate their mutational scars.

01

APOBEC3 Protein Sectors

Using statistical coupling analysis and co-evolutionary methods to identify functional sectors within APOBEC3 proteins — mapping how co-evolving residue networks encode distinct biological functions.

02

Mutational Signature Analysis

Building trinucleotide-context pipelines that quantify how often each genomic context mutates across viral and tumour sequences — revealing the fingerprints of mutational processes.

03

Deep Learning for Genomics

Applying neural networks to identify antimicrobial peptides from the human gut microbiome and to model complex sequence–function relationships in pathogen biology.

04

Open Source Tools

Developing and publishing reproducible, pip-installable computational tools — including nContextMut — so the community can build on this work directly.
